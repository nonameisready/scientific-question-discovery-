\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Automated scientific discovery.}
Systems from early hypothesis-formation programs to contemporary
``AI scientist'' pipelines aim to automate stages of the research loop.
Most target hypothesis generation, experiment design, or paper writing
downstream of an already-chosen question; the selection of \emph{which}
question deserves the loop is usually left to the human operator or an
unexamined prompt. \todo{cite: BACON/AM lineage, robot scientist (Adam/Eve),
recent LLM agent-scientist systems}

\paragraph{Open-ended learning and curiosity.}
Intrinsic-motivation and novelty-search methods formalize ``interestingness''
for agents exploring environments. Our setting differs in that the
environment is the scientific record itself: interestingness must be
grounded in evidential structure (contradiction, unexplained variance,
single-source support) rather than state-visitation statistics.
\todo{cite: intrinsic motivation, novelty search, open-endedness}

\paragraph{LLMs for science.}
Large language models are increasingly used for literature synthesis,
claim verification, and research ideation. Ideation studies typically
evaluate novelty and feasibility by human panels at generation time; our
historical validation instead scores questions by what the field actually
did afterwards, which removes the panel's guess about the future from the
measurement. \todo{cite: scientific claim verification, LLM ideation
evaluations, LLM-as-judge caveats}

\paragraph{Claim extraction and scientific knowledge graphs.}
Our evidence graph follows work on typed scientific claim
representations, with two deliberate departures: every claim carries its
source location and verification tier, and tension edges between claims
are typed (\emph{contradicts, qualifies, challenges-method,
explains-discrepancy, complements, supports}) after a human review pass
showed that collapsing all tension into ``contradiction'' wrongly
upgrades limiting conditions into refutations
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments:tension}). \todo{cite: SciFact, knowledge
graph construction from literature}
