## q_001 (P1 — observational_tension)

**Under what atmospheric conditions does HD 189733 b exhibit strong vertical wind shear between the high-altitude sodium-traced layers (showing ~8 km/s blueshift) and the deeper CO/H2O-traced layers (showing ~1.7 km/s wind), as inferred from simultaneous or contemporaneous multi-wavelength spectroscopy?**

- rationale: The evidence shows a significant discrepancy in measured wind velocities from different spectral tracers and altitudes, suggesting vertical wind shear as a possible mechanism. Understanding the atmospheric conditions that produce this shear is crucial for interpreting exoplanet wind measurements and for constraining atmospheric dynamics models.
- test sketch: Archival or near-term observations using both optical (sodium) and near-infrared (CO/H2O) high-resolution spectroscopy, ideally obtained simultaneously or during similar orbital phases, could be analyzed to correlate wind shear with atmospheric temperature, pressure, or irradiation conditions.
- objects: HD 189733 b
- papers: 2015A&A...577A..62W, 2016ApJ...817..106B
- archival spectra: HD189733:HST=271

## q_002 (P2 — methodological_challenge)

**Under what atmospheric heterogeneity conditions do one-dimensional retrievals of WASP-12 b's transmission spectrum yield water abundance estimates that are robust (within a factor of two) to the biases identified in multi-dimensional retrieval experiments?**

- rationale: The 2015 detection of water in WASP-12 b's atmosphere is robust, but the 2020 study shows that one-dimensional retrievals can significantly bias inferred water abundances in the presence of heterogeneous terminators. Understanding the specific heterogeneity conditions under which abundance estimates remain reliable is crucial for interpreting current and future retrievals.
- test sketch: Archival HST/WFC3 spectra of WASP-12 b can be reanalyzed using multi-dimensional retrieval frameworks to compare inferred water abundances across a range of assumed terminator heterogeneity scenarios.
- objects: WASP-12 b, WASP-17 b
- papers: 2015ApJ...814...66K, 2020ApJ...893L..43M
- archival spectra: WASP-12:HST=2643, WASP-17:HST=295, WASP-17:JWST=6

## q_003 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

Understanding the robustness of the water feature to atmospheric parameters such as clouds is crucial for interpreting transmission spectra and constraining atmospheric models.

- archival spectra: WASP-12:HST=2643

## q_004 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

- papers: 2015ApJ...814...66K
- archival spectra: WASP-12:HST=2643

## q_005 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

**Under what cloud inhomogeneity conditions does the detection significance of nitrogen-bearing species (NH3 and/or HCN) in the atmosphere of HD 209458 b, as inferred from HST transmission spectra, remain robust above 3 sigma?**

- rationale: The evidence shows that the detection significance for NH3 and/or HCN varies depending on the assumed cloud properties, with significance ranging from 3.3 to 7.7 sigma. This suggests that cloud inhomogeneity could impact the reliability of nitrogen chemistry detections, making it important to delineate the conditions under which these detections are robust.
- test sketch: A systematic reanalysis of the HST transmission spectrum using retrievals with a grid of cloud inhomogeneity models can map the detection significance of NH3 and HCN as a function of cloud properties.
- objects: HD 209458 b
- papers: 2017MNRAS.469.1979M
- archival spectra: HD209458:HST=1556

## q_006 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

## q_007 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

**Under what atmospheric conditions do patchy cloud models with solar composition provide robust fits to HST/WFC3 spectra of hot Jupiter and Neptune-class exoplanets, without invoking high mean molecular weight or globally uniform clouds?**

- rationale: The evidence shows that for HD 189733 b and HAT-P-11 b, partially cloudy solar-composition models fit the HST/WFC3 spectra well, suggesting that such models may be sufficient under certain conditions. Understanding the limits of this explanation is important for interpreting spectra of similar exoplanets and distinguishing between cloud properties and atmospheric composition.
- test sketch: A systematic retrieval analysis of HST/WFC3 spectra for a larger sample of hot Jupiter and Neptune-class exoplanets can identify the range of atmospheric parameters where patchy cloud solar-composition models remain consistent with observations.
- objects: HAT-P-11 b, HD 189733 b
- papers: 2016ApJ...820...78L
- archival spectra: HAT-P-11:HST=664, HD189733:HST=271

## q_009 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

**Under what atmospheric compositions and temperature-pressure profiles are carbon-dioxide absorption features in TRAPPIST-1 planets robustly detectable by JWST/NIRSpec Prism transmission spectroscopy within ten transits, assuming aerosol-free conditions?**

- rationale: The evidence shows that simulated JWST/NIRSpec Prism observations predict CO2-rich atmospheres could be detected in fewer than ten transits for all TRAPPIST-1 planets if high-altitude aerosols are absent. However, the detectability may depend on specific atmospheric compositions and temperature-pressure structures, making it important to delineate these conditions.
- test sketch: Archival and future JWST/NIRSpec Prism observations of TRAPPIST-1 planets can be compared to simulated spectra across a range of plausible atmospheric models to determine under which conditions CO2 features are detectable within the predicted transit count.
- objects: TRAPPIST-1 b, TRAPPIST-1 c, TRAPPIST-1 d, TRAPPIST-1 e, TRAPPIST-1 f, TRAPPIST-1 g, TRAPPIST-1 h
- papers: 2019AJ....158...27L
- archival spectra: TRAPPIST-1:HST=6
