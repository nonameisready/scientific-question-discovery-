## q_001 (P1 — observational_tension)

**Under what atmospheric conditions does HD 189733 b exhibit strong vertical wind shear between the high-altitude sodium-traced layers (showing ~8 km/s blueshift) and the deeper CO/H2O-traced layers (showing ~1.7 km/s wind), as inferred from simultaneous or contemporaneous multi-wavelength spectroscopy?**

- rationale: The evidence shows a substantial discrepancy in measured wind velocities from different spectral tracers and altitudes, suggesting vertical wind shear as a possible mechanism. Understanding the atmospheric conditions that produce this shear is crucial for interpreting exoplanet wind measurements and for constraining atmospheric dynamics models.
- test sketch: Archival or new simultaneous high-resolution optical (sodium) and near-infrared (CO/H2O) spectra of HD 189733 b, analyzed for wind velocities and temperature profiles, could be compared to atmospheric models to identify the conditions under which such wind shear arises.
- objects: HD 189733 b
- papers: 2015A&A...577A..62W, 2016ApJ...817..106B
- archival spectra: HD189733:HST=271

## q_002 (P2 — methodological_challenge)

**Under what atmospheric heterogeneity conditions do one-dimensional retrievals of WASP-12 b's transmission spectrum yield robust water detection significance, even if the inferred abundance is biased?**

- rationale: The 2015 study reports a high-confidence water detection in WASP-12 b using one-dimensional retrievals, while the 2020 study demonstrates that such retrievals can significantly bias abundance estimates in the presence of heterogeneous terminators. This raises the question of whether the detection significance itself is robust to heterogeneity, or if only the abundance values are affected.
- test sketch: Archival HST/WFC3 spectra of WASP-12 b can be reanalyzed using multi-dimensional retrieval frameworks to compare detection significances and assess the impact of assumed atmospheric heterogeneity.
- objects: WASP-12 b, WASP-17 b
- papers: 2015ApJ...814...66K, 2020ApJ...893L..43M
- archival spectra: WASP-12:HST=2643, WASP-17:HST=295, WASP-17:JWST=6

## q_003 (P2 — methodological_challenge)

**Under what atmospheric heterogeneity conditions does the one-dimensional retrieval method for WASP-12 b's transmission spectrum yield water volume mixing ratios that are robust to biases exceeding an order of magnitude?**

- rationale: The evidence shows that one-dimensional retrievals can significantly bias molecular abundance estimates, particularly for water, when heterogeneous terminators are present, as demonstrated for WASP-12 b. This challenges the reliability of previously inferred water mixing ratios and their implications for the planet's C/O ratio, making it important to identify the specific heterogeneity conditions under which these biases become significant.
- test sketch: Comparative retrieval analyses using both one-dimensional and multi-dimensional models on archival HST/WFC3 spectra of WASP-12 b, combined with synthetic spectra simulating varying degrees of terminator heterogeneity, could quantify the robustness of water abundance estimates.
- objects: WASP-12 b, WASP-17 b
- papers: 2015ApJ...814...66K, 2020ApJ...893L..43M
- archival spectra: WASP-12:HST=2643, WASP-17:JWST=6, WASP-17:HST=295

## q_004 (P3 — independent_qualification)

**Under what atmospheric conditions does HD 189733 b exhibit subsolar water abundances alongside robust carbon monoxide detection in high-dispersion transmission spectra?**

- rationale: Both analyses using VLT/CRIRES data consistently detect carbon monoxide in HD 189733 b, but the later retrieval framework interprets the same spectra as indicating subsolar water abundances. This suggests a need to clarify the atmospheric mechanisms or retrieval sensitivities that could produce this molecular abundance pattern.
- test sketch: Archival and future high-resolution transmission spectra of HD 189733 b can be reanalyzed with varied retrieval models and compared to explore how inferred water abundances depend on atmospheric assumptions and data quality.
- objects: HD 189733 b, HD 209458 b
- papers: 2016ApJ...817..106B, 2019AJ....157..114B
- archival spectra: HD189733:HST=271, HD209458:HST=1556

## q_005 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

**Under what atmospheric temperature and pressure conditions does the water absorption feature observed in the HST/WFC3 transmission spectrum of WASP-12 b remain robust against variations in cloud opacity and composition?**

- rationale: The high-confidence detection of water absorption in WASP-12 b's atmosphere using HST/WFC3 motivates investigation into the physical conditions that preserve this spectral feature. Understanding the robustness of the water signal to atmospheric parameters such as temperature, pressure, and cloud properties is essential for interpreting transmission spectra and constraining atmospheric models.
- test sketch: Archival HST/WFC3 spectra can be reanalyzed with atmospheric retrieval models that systematically vary temperature, pressure, and cloud parameters to determine the range of conditions consistent with the observed water absorption feature.
- objects: WASP-12 b
- papers: 2015ApJ...814...66K
- archival spectra: WASP-12:HST=2643

## q_006 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

**Under what conditions does the equilibrium-chemistry retrieval of WASP-12 b's HST/WFC3 spectrum robustly exclude a carbon-to-oxygen ratio (C/O) greater than 1 at more than 3 sigma confidence?**

- rationale: The evidence shows that a specific retrieval approach using HST/WFC3 data for WASP-12 b excludes high C/O values, but this result depends on the assumptions of equilibrium chemistry and the parameterization used. Understanding the robustness of this exclusion is important for interpreting atmospheric composition and for comparing with results from other retrieval models or datasets.
- test sketch: Reanalyze the HST/WFC3 spectrum of WASP-12 b using alternative retrieval frameworks (e.g., non-equilibrium chemistry, different priors) and compare the resulting C/O constraints to assess the sensitivity of the exclusion to model assumptions.
- objects: WASP-12 b
- papers: 2015ApJ...814...66K
- archival spectra: WASP-12:HST=2643

## q_007 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

**Under what cloud inhomogeneity conditions does the detection significance of nitrogen-bearing species (NH3 and/or HCN) in the atmosphere of HD 209458 b, as inferred from HST transmission spectra, remain robust above 3 sigma?**

- rationale: The evidence shows that the detection significance for NH3 and/or HCN varies (3.3–7.7 sigma) depending on the assumed cloud properties in two-dimensional retrievals. This suggests that the inferred presence of nitrogen chemistry may be sensitive to the treatment of inhomogeneous clouds, making it important to test the robustness of these detections to different cloud scenarios.
- test sketch: Reanalyze the HST transmission spectrum of HD 209458 b using retrieval models with systematically varied cloud inhomogeneity parameters to map how detection significance for NH3 and HCN changes.
- objects: HD 209458 b
- papers: 2017MNRAS.469.1979M
- archival spectra: HD209458:HST=1556

## q_008 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

**Under what atmospheric conditions does HD 209458 b exhibit strongly subsolar water abundance (0.01-0.03 times solar) at the terminator, as inferred from HST transmission spectra with evidence for non-uniform cloud coverage and high-altitude haze?**

- rationale: The HST transmission spectrum analysis for HD 209458 b robustly supports a model with non-uniform clouds, high-altitude haze, and a significantly subsolar water abundance at the terminator. Understanding the atmospheric mechanisms or conditions that produce such low water abundances in the presence of clouds and haze is crucial for interpreting exoplanet spectra and atmospheric chemistry.
- test sketch: Comparative retrievals using archival and future transmission spectra at multiple wavelengths, especially with higher spectral resolution or broader wavelength coverage, could constrain the interplay between cloud/haze properties and water abundance in HD 209458 b's atmosphere.
- objects: HD 209458 b
- papers: 2017MNRAS.469.1979M
- archival spectra: HD209458:HST=1556

## q_009 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

**Under what atmospheric conditions do patchy cloud models with solar composition provide robust fits to HST/WFC3 spectra of hot Neptunes and hot Jupiters, without invoking high mean molecular weight or globally uniform clouds?**

- rationale: The evidence shows that for HD 189733 b and HAT-P-11 b, partially cloudy solar-composition models fit the HST/WFC3 spectra well, suggesting that neither high mean molecular weight nor uniform clouds are necessary for these cases. Investigating the specific atmospheric parameters (e.g., temperature, cloud coverage fraction, metallicity) that allow such models to succeed could clarify the generality of this explanation across similar exoplanets.
- test sketch: A systematic retrieval analysis of HST/WFC3 spectra for a broader sample of hot Neptunes and hot Jupiters can map the parameter space where patchy cloud, solar-composition models remain consistent with observations.
- objects: HAT-P-11 b, HD 189733 b
- papers: 2016ApJ...820...78L
- archival spectra: HAT-P-11:HST=664, HD189733:HST=271

## q_011 (P4 — single_dataset_conclusion)

**Under what atmospheric compositions and temperature-pressure profiles are carbon-dioxide absorption features in TRAPPIST-1 planets robustly detectable by JWST/NIRSpec Prism transmission spectroscopy within ten transits, assuming aerosol-free conditions?**

- rationale: The evidence shows that simulated JWST/NIRSpec Prism observations predict CO2-rich atmospheres could be detected in fewer than ten transits for all TRAPPIST-1 planets if high-altitude aerosols are absent. However, the detectability may depend on specific atmospheric compositions and temperature-pressure structures, making it important to delineate these conditions.
- test sketch: Archival and future JWST/NIRSpec Prism observations of TRAPPIST-1 planets can be compared to simulated spectra across a range of plausible atmospheric models to determine under which conditions CO2 features are detectable within the predicted transit count.
- objects: TRAPPIST-1 b, TRAPPIST-1 c, TRAPPIST-1 d, TRAPPIST-1 e, TRAPPIST-1 f, TRAPPIST-1 g, TRAPPIST-1 h
- papers: 2019AJ....158...27L
- archival spectra: TRAPPIST-1:HST=6
