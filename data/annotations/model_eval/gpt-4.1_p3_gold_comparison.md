# Gold vs Model Claim Comparison

model recovered the human claims (coverage) and asserted anything the

## 2015A&A...573A.122W

**Gold (human):**
- Small sub-micron condensate particles can produce prominent infrared vibrational features accompanied by an optical scattering slope in hot-Jupiter transmission spectra.
- Long-wavelength vibrational features may distinguish condensate-cloud formation from photochemical-haze scenarios even when individual cloud species remain degenerate.

**Model:**
- Significant infrared absorption features appear in computed transmission spectra of hot Jupiter exoplanets due to vibrational modes between key species in each cloud condensate.
- Vibrational mode features in the infrared are prominent when clouds are composed of small sub-micron sized particles and are associated with an optical scattering slope.
- It may be possible to discern different vibrational bonds in transmission spectra, which can distinguish between cloud formation scenarios such as condensate clouds and photochemically generated species.

## 2015A&A...577A..62W

**Gold (human):**
- High-resolution HARPS transmission spectroscopy resolves the sodium D doublet in the atmosphere of HD 189733 b at high significance.
- The resolved sodium lines in HD 189733 b indicate hotter upper-atmosphere layers and a possible high-altitude wind blueshift.

**Model:**
- HARPS transmission spectroscopy spectrally resolves the sodium (Na I) D doublet in the atmosphere of HD 189733 b, measuring line contrasts of 0.64 ± 0.07% (D2) and 0.40 ± 0.07% (D1) and FWHMs of 0.52 ± 0.08 Å, corresponding to a 10σ detection of excess absorption.
- The resolved sodium line cores indicate atmospheric temperatures of 2600 ± 600 K (D1) and 3270 ± 330 K (D2) at altitudes of 9800 ± 2800 km and 12,700 ± 2600 km, respectively, with a measured temperature gradient of ~0.2 K km⁻¹ in the sodium-dominated region.
- A blueshift of 0.16 ± 0.04 Å (4σ) is detected in the sodium line positions, consistent with winds blowing at 8 ± 2 km s⁻¹ in the upper atmosphere.
- High-resolution spectrographs on 4-m-class telescopes are demonstrated to be effective for detailed characterization of exoplanet atmospheres.

## 2015ApJ...813...13W

**Gold (human):**
- A two-stage hybrid temperature-pressure retrieval can combine the convergence robustness of a parametric profile with the accuracy of a layer-by-layer profile for exoplanet emission spectra.

**Model:**
- The T-REx hybrid model introduces a new two-stage temperature-pressure (TP) profile retrieval for exoplanet emission spectra that combines the robustness of analytic parametric solutions with the accuracy of layer-by-layer approaches.
- Emission spectroscopy retrievals are significantly more degenerate than transmission spectroscopy due to the need to retrieve the full atmospheric TP profile, especially with current low signal-to-noise and low spectral resolution data.
- The T-REx hybrid retrieval process is demonstrated on simulated spectra of WASP-76 b and 55 Cnc e, as well as on secondary eclipse measurements of HD 189733 b.

## 2015ApJ...814...66K

**Gold (human):**
- The HST/WFC3 transmission spectrum of WASP-12 b contains a high-confidence water absorption signal.
- An equilibrium-chemistry retrieval of WASP-12 b favors a near-solar carbon-to-oxygen ratio and rules out a carbon-rich atmosphere.

**Model:**
- HST/WFC3 transmission spectroscopy detects strong water absorption in the atmosphere of WASP-12 b at 7σ confidence, marking the first high-confidence spectroscopic identification of a molecule in this planet's atmosphere.
- Retrievals using the transmission spectrum yield a 1σ water volume mixing ratio between 10^-5 and 10^-2, which is consistent with a carbon-to-oxygen ratio (C/O) greater than 1 to within 2σ.
- A new retrieval parameterization that fits for C/O and metallicity under the assumption of chemical equilibrium constrains WASP-12 b's C/O to 0.5 (+0.2, -0.3) at 1σ and rules out a carbon-rich atmosphere (C/O > 1) at greater than 3σ confidence.
- The new transmission spectrum results are in tension with previous dayside emission measurements that suggested a carbon-rich atmosphere for WASP-12 b.

## 2016ApJ...817...17G

**Gold (human):**
- For clear solar-composition transiting exoplanet atmospheres, JWST transmission spectra from 1 to 2.5 microns can often constrain the major molecular constituents.
- Cloudy or high-mean-molecular-weight atmospheres generally require broader 1 to 11 micron JWST coverage than clear atmospheres to obtain good constraints.

**Model:**
- JWST transmission spectra from 1–2.5 μm will often constrain the major molecular constituents of clear, solar-composition exoplanet atmospheres well.
- Cloudy or high mean molecular weight (HMMW) exoplanet atmospheres will often require full 1–11 μm spectra for good constraints on atmospheric properties, and emission data may be more useful for planets with sufficiently high flux ratios.
- Strong temperature inversions in solar-composition hot Jupiter atmospheres should be detectable with 1–2.5+ μm emission spectra, and 1–5+ μm emission spectra will constrain the temperature-pressure profiles of warm planets.
- Transmission spectra over 1–5+ μm will constrain atmospheric metallicity ([Fe/H]) to better than 0.5 dex for the clear atmospheres of the hot and warm planets studied.
- Carbon-to-oxygen ratios can be constrained to better than a factor of 2 in some exoplanet systems using JWST spectra.

## 2016ApJ...817..106B

**Gold (human):**
- High-dispersion transmission spectroscopy detects carbon monoxide and water vapor in HD 189733 b and yields a rotation rate consistent with tidal locking.
- The near-infrared data indicate a small day-to-night wind in HD 189733 b and, when compared with a much larger optical sodium blueshift, imply strong vertical wind shear.

**Model:**
- High-dispersion transmission spectroscopy detects rotational broadening in the atmosphere of HD 189733 b, corresponding to a rotational velocity of (3.4<sub>-2.1</sub><sup>+1.3</sup>) km s<sup>-1</sup> and a rotational period of (1.7<sub>-0.4</sub><sup>+2.9</sup>) days, consistent with tidal locking to its 2.2-day orbit.
- The rotation of HD 189733 b is longer than 1 day at 3σ confidence.
- The data only marginally (1.5σ) prefer models with planetary rotation over models without rotation.
- A small day- to night-side wind speed of (-1.7<sub>-1.2</sub><sup>+1.1</sup>) km s<sup>-1</sup> is measured in the atmosphere of HD 189733 b, implying a strong vertical wind shear compared to previously detected sodium blueshifts.

## 2016ApJ...820...78L

**Gold (human):**
- Within the HST/WFC3 bandpass, fractional cloud coverage can exactly mimic a cloud-free high-mean-molecular-weight atmosphere.
- The HST/WFC3 spectra of HD 189733 b and HAT-P-11 b can be explained by solar-composition atmospheres with patchy clouds without requiring high mean molecular weight or globally uniform clouds.
- Rayleigh-scattering differences or ingress-egress residuals can break the degeneracy between patchy solar-composition clouds and high-mean-molecular-weight atmospheres.

**Model:**
- Fractional (patchy) cloud coverage in exoplanet atmospheres can exactly mimic the transmission spectra of high mean molecular weight atmospheres over certain wavelength regions, particularly in the HST/WFC3 bandpass (1.1–1.7 μm), and vice versa.
- Patchy cloud coverage produces a distinct spectral signature compared to uniform global clouds in transit transmission spectra.
- Atmospheric retrievals on both synthetic and real exoplanets show that cloud-free high mean molecular weight atmospheres and partially cloudy solar composition atmospheres can explain the same transmission spectra equally well, demonstrating a degeneracy in interpretation.
- The HST/WFC3 transmission spectra of HD 189733 b and HAT-P-11 b can be explained by solar composition atmospheres with patchy clouds, without requiring high mean molecular weight or global clouds.
- The degeneracy between high mean molecular weight and solar composition partially cloudy atmospheres can be broken by observing differences in molecular Rayleigh scattering, and partially cloudy limbs produce a ~100 ppm residual in transit ingress and egress if transit timing is known to seconds.

## 2016ApJ...822L...4E

**Gold (human):**
- HST/WFC3 transmission spectroscopy detects the 1.4 micron water band in WASP-121 b at high confidence.
- The deeper optical transits of WASP-121 b are unlikely to be caused by high-altitude haze alone and are consistent with additional TiO and VO opacity.

**Model:**
- HST/WFC3 transmission spectroscopy detects the 1.4 μm water absorption band at high confidence in the atmosphere of WASP-121 b.
- Significantly deeper optical transits relative to near-infrared wavelengths in WASP-121 b are interpreted as evidence for titanium oxide and vanadium oxide absorption rather than high-altitude haze.
- Enhanced opacity across the 1.12-1.3 μm wavelength range in WASP-121 b's atmosphere is possibly due to iron hydride absorption.
- The presence of titanium oxide, vanadium oxide, and iron hydride in WASP-121 b's atmosphere is likely to significantly affect its atmospheric physics and chemistry, including the production of a strong thermal inversion.

## 2017MNRAS.469.1979M

**Gold (human):**
- A two-dimensional retrieval with inhomogeneous clouds finds evidence for nitrogen-bearing chemistry in the atmosphere of HD 209458 b.
- The preferred retrieval for HD 209458 b indicates non-uniform cloud coverage, high-altitude haze, and strongly subsolar water abundance at the terminator.

**Model:**
- Transmission spectrum retrievals for HD 209458b provide the first detection of nitrogen chemistry (NH3 and/or HCN) in an exoplanet atmosphere at 3.7–7.7σ confidence.
- HD 209458b's atmosphere exhibits non-uniform (patchy) cloud coverage at the day-night terminator at 4.5–5.4σ confidence.
- The water abundance in HD 209458b's atmosphere is sub-solar, constrained to 5–15 ppm (0.01–0.03 × solar) at ≳3–5σ, depending on cloud distribution assumptions.
- The detection of NH3 and sub-solar H2O in HD 209458b's atmosphere strongly suggests disequilibrium chemistry and cautions against assuming chemical/radiative equilibrium in retrievals.

## 2018ApJ...853..122R

**Gold (human):**
- Stellar heterogeneity in M dwarfs can imprint contamination signals in rocky-planet transmission spectra that exceed the expected planetary atmospheric features by more than an order of magnitude.
- For the TRAPPIST-1 system, stellar contamination can alter atmospheric-species transit depths by roughly 1 to 15 times the expected planetary signal and bias inferred planet densities low.

**Model:**
- Stellar contamination from spots and faculae on M dwarfs can be more than 10 times larger than the transit depth changes expected for atmospheric features in rocky exoplanets, potentially overwhelming planetary atmospheric signals in transmission spectra.
- Corrections that assume a linear relation between photometric variability amplitude and spot/faculae covering fractions generally underestimate the level of stellar contamination in transmission spectra.
- Stellar spectral contamination can lead to systematic errors in the measured radius and derived density of small transiting planets, resulting in underestimates of bulk density and overestimates of volatile content.
- For the TRAPPIST-1 system, spot covering fractions of 8% (−7%/+18%) and faculae covering fractions of 54% (−46%/+16%) are consistent with the observed rotational variability, and the resulting stellar contamination alters transit depths at wavelengths of interest for planetary atmospheric species by roughly 1–15 times the strength of planetary features.
- Stellar contamination in the TRAPPIST-1 system can lead to underestimates of the bulk densities of its planets by −8% (−20%/+7%), which would result in overestimates of their volatile contents.

## 2018NatAs...2..303H

**Gold (human):**
- Laboratory simulations show that all tested super-Earth and mini-Neptune atmospheric compositions produce haze particles, but with production rates spanning orders of magnitude.
- Cooler 1,000-times-solar, water-dominated or methane-rich atmospheres can produce haze at rates exceeding the authors' Titan reference experiment.

**Model:**
- Laboratory simulations of super-Earth and mini-Neptune atmospheres across a range of temperatures (300–600 K) and metallicities (100×, 1,000×, 10,000× solar) demonstrate that all tested atmospheric compositions can produce haze particles.
- Haze production rates in cooler (300 and 400 K), 1,000× solar metallicity atmospheres can exceed those observed in standard Titan simulations, reaching up to 10 mg h⁻¹ compared to Titan's 7.4 mg h⁻¹.
- There is substantial diversity in haze production rates among super-Earth and mini-Neptune atmospheres, with some atmospheres producing significant photochemical haze and others producing very little.

## 2019AJ....157...96R

**Gold (human):**
- Typical stellar heterogeneity in inactive FGK dwarfs is unlikely to produce detectable molecular-band contamination in transmission spectra, whereas active G and K hosts can produce detectable visual slopes and line-region effects.

**Model:**
- Stellar contamination spectra from unocculted spots and faculae are considerably weaker for FGK dwarfs than for typical M dwarfs, due to lower spot covering fractions implied by their typical variabilities.
- Typically active G and K dwarfs can produce visual slopes in transmission spectra that are detectable with high-precision observations.
- Unocculted faculae in K dwarfs can appreciably alter transit depths around the Na D doublet in transmission spectra.
- Band-averaged transit depth offsets at molecular bands for CH₄, CO, CO₂, H₂O, N₂O, O₂, and O₃ are not detectable for typically active FGK dwarfs, but stellar TiO/VO features may be detectable for typically active late-K dwarfs.
- Inactive FGK dwarfs do not produce detectable stellar contamination features in transmission spectra, but active FGK host stars can produce such features, necessitating caution in interpreting transmission spectra from these systems.

## 2019AJ....158...27L

- For aerosol-free TRAPPIST-1 planets, JWST/NIRSpec Prism transmission spectroscopy could detect carbon-dioxide-containing terrestrial atmospheres in fewer than ten transits.
- Water and biogenic oxygen or ozone would be extremely difficult to detect in plausible TRAPPIST-1 atmospheres, while abiotic oxygen from past ocean loss may be more accessible through collision-induced oxygen or near-infrared ozone features.

## 2019MNRAS.482.1485P

**Gold (human):**
- A homogeneous retrieval analysis finds that most of the ten hot Jupiters studied have day-night terminator water abundances consistent with subsolar values.
- The retrieved hot-Jupiter water abundances imply either subsolar oxygen abundance, supersolar carbon-to-oxygen ratio, or a combination of both.

- The retrieved atmospheric properties provide important constraints on the formation and migration pathways of hot giant exoplanets.

## 2020A&A...635A.206C

**Gold (human):**
- The combined HARPS-N and CARMENES transmission spectra of HD 209458 b show no detectable sodium absorption once center-to-limb variation and the Rossiter-McLaughlin effect are modeled.
- Some previously reported high-resolution atmospheric detections may require revision because stellar center-to-limb variation and Rossiter-McLaughlin signatures can mimic planetary absorption.

**Model:**
- The transmission spectrum of HD 209458b reveals no detectable Na I absorption, contrary to previous reports of atmospheric sodium detection.
- The observed transmission spectra, including strong lines such as Na I, Hα, Ca II IRT, Mg I triplet, and K I D1, can be explained by the combination of centre-to-limb variation and the Rossiter-McLaughlin effect without invoking any exoplanet atmospheric contribution.
- Previous studies of HD 209458b may have overlooked the impact of centre-to-limb variation and the Rossiter-McLaughlin effect, potentially leading to spurious detections of atmospheric features.
- For some star-planet systems, the effects of centre-to-limb variation and the Rossiter-McLaughlin effect are significant enough that published atmospheric detections may require revision.

## 2020ApJ...890..174K

**Gold (human):**
- Helios-r2 retrievals can recover surface gravities for benchmark brown dwarfs that agree with previous studies or dynamical-mass constraints.
- Retrieved brown-dwarf radii and masses can be unphysical or model dependent, and inferred carbon-to-oxygen ratio and metallicity depend on whether chemical equilibrium is assumed.

**Model:**
- Helios-r2, a new open-source Bayesian retrieval code, successfully retrieves atmospheric parameters from medium-resolution emission spectra of brown dwarfs, as demonstrated by tests on both self-consistent and Sonora-model-generated synthetic spectra.
- A calibration factor included in Helios-r2 to account for uncertainties in brown dwarf radius, distance, and flux calibration can sometimes erroneously compensate for discrepancies in modeling choices and implementation.
- Retrieved surface gravities for GJ 570 D and Epsilon Indi Ba and Bb using Helios-r2 are consistent with previous studies and/or values inferred from dynamical masses.
- There is no clear criterion for rejecting unphysical values of retrieved brown dwarf radii, so inferred radii and corresponding masses from Helios-r2 should be treated with caution.
- Retrieved carbon-to-oxygen ratios and metallicity from Helios-r2 depend on whether chemical equilibrium is assumed in the retrieval.

## 2020ApJ...893L..43M

**Gold (human):**
- Applying one-dimensional retrieval models to spectra from atmospheres with different morning and evening terminator compositions can produce spuriously cold retrieved temperatures.
- One-dimensional retrievals of heterogeneous terminators can bias retrieved molecular abundances, including water, by more than an order of magnitude.

**Model:**
- Erroneously cold temperatures are retrieved from exoplanet transmission spectra when 1D atmospheric models are applied to planets with differing morning-evening terminator compositions.
- 1D retrieval techniques artificially tune atmospheric parameters away from terminator-averaged properties, resulting in temperature profiles that are hundreds of degrees cooler and have weaker temperature gradients than reality.
- Retrieved abundances from 1D models are mostly biased by more than 1σ and sometimes by more than 3σ, with the most extreme biases occurring for ultra-hot Jupiters.
- When morning-evening compositional differences affect prominent opacity sources, H2O abundances retrieved by 1D models can be biased by over an order of magnitude.
- The cold retrieved temperatures reported for WASP-17 b and WASP-12 b can be explained by biases introduced by 1D atmospheric models.
