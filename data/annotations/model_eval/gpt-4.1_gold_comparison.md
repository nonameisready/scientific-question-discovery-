## 2015A&A...573A.122W

**Gold (human):**
- Small sub-micron condensate particles can produce prominent infrared vibrational features accompanied by an optical scattering slope in hot-Jupiter transmission spectra.
- Long-wavelength vibrational features may distinguish condensate-cloud formation from photochemical-haze scenarios even when individual cloud species remain degenerate.

**Model:**
- Significant infrared absorption features appear in the computed transmission spectrum of hot Jupiter exoplanets, resulting from vibrational modes between key species in each condensate.
- Vibrational mode features are prominent in the infrared when clouds are composed of small sub-micron sized particles and are associated with an accompanying optical scattering slope.

## 2015A&A...577A..62W

**Gold (human):**
- High-resolution HARPS transmission spectroscopy resolves the sodium D doublet in the atmosphere of HD 189733 b at high significance.
- The resolved sodium lines in HD 189733 b indicate hotter upper-atmosphere layers and a possible high-altitude wind blueshift.

**Model:**
- We spectrally resolve the Na I D doublet in the atmosphere of HD 189733b and measure line contrasts of 0.64 ± 0.07% (D2) and 0.40 ± 0.07% (D1) with FWHMs of 0.52 ± 0.08 Å.
- We derive temperatures of 2600 ± 600 K and 3270 ± 330 K at altitudes of 9800 ± 2800 km and 12,700 ± 2600 km in the Na I D1 and D2 line cores, respectively, in the atmosphere of HD 189733b.
- We measure a temperature gradient of approximately 0.2 K km⁻¹ in the region where sodium absorption dominates over haze absorption in the atmosphere of HD 189733b.
- We detect a blueshift of 0.16 ± 0.04 Å (4σ) in the Na I line positions, which may indicate winds blowing at 8 ± 2 km s⁻¹ in the upper atmosphere of HD 189733b.

## 2015ApJ...814...66K

**Model:**

## 2016ApJ...817...17G

**Gold (human):**
- For clear solar-composition transiting exoplanet atmospheres, JWST transmission spectra from 1 to 2.5 microns can often constrain the major molecular constituents.
- Cloudy or high-mean-molecular-weight atmospheres generally require broader 1 to 11 micron JWST coverage than clear atmospheres to obtain good constraints.

**Model:**

- Cloudy or high mean molecular weight (HMMW) exoplanet atmospheres will often require full 1-11 μm JWST spectra for good constraints on atmospheric properties.
- Strong temperature inversions in solar-composition hot-Jupiter atmospheres should be detectable with 1-2.5+ μm JWST emission spectra.
- Transmission spectra over 1-5+ μm from JWST will constrain [Fe/H] values to better than 0.5 dex for the clear atmospheres of the hot and warm planets studied.

## 2016ApJ...820...78L

**Gold (human):**
- Within the HST/WFC3 bandpass, fractional cloud coverage can exactly mimic a cloud-free high-mean-molecular-weight atmosphere.
- The HST/WFC3 spectra of HD 189733 b and HAT-P-11 b can be explained by solar-composition atmospheres with patchy clouds without requiring high mean molecular weight or globally uniform clouds.
- Rayleigh-scattering differences or ingress-egress residuals can break the degeneracy between patchy solar-composition clouds and high-mean-molecular-weight atmospheres.

**Model:**
- Fractional (patchy) cloud coverage can exactly mimic high mean molecular weight atmospheres and vice versa over certain wavelength regions, particularly over the HST/WFC3 bandpass (1.1-1.7 μm).
- The HST/WFC3 transit transmission spectra of HD 189733 b and HAT-P-11 b can be explained well by solar composition atmospheres with patchy clouds, without invoking high mean molecular weight or global clouds.
- The degeneracy between high molecular weight and solar composition partially cloudy atmospheres can be broken by observing the molecular Rayleigh scattering differences between the two.
- The signature of partially cloudy limbs appears as an approximately 100 ppm residual in the ingress and egress of transit light curves, provided that the transit timing is known to seconds.

## 2018NatAs...2..303H

**Gold (human):**
- Laboratory simulations show that all tested super-Earth and mini-Neptune atmospheric compositions produce haze particles, but with production rates spanning orders of magnitude.
- Cooler 1,000-times-solar, water-dominated or methane-rich atmospheres can produce haze at rates exceeding the authors' Titan reference experiment.

**Model:**
- All simulated atmospheres in the laboratory experiments produced particles.
- Cooler (300 and 400 K), 1,000× solar metallicity atmospheres exhibited haze production rates higher than the standard Titan simulation.
- Particle production rates varied greatly among the simulated atmospheres, with some as low as 0.04 mg h⁻¹ for the case with 100× solar metallicity at 600 K.
- There is great diversity in haze production rates, and some—but not all—super-Earth and mini-Neptune atmospheres will possess photochemically generated haze.

## 2019AJ....157...96R

**Gold (human):**
- Typical stellar heterogeneity in inactive FGK dwarfs is unlikely to produce detectable molecular-band contamination in transmission spectra, whereas active G and K hosts can produce detectable visual slopes and line-region effects.

**Model:**
- Typically active G and K dwarfs produce visual slopes in transmission spectra that are detectable in high-precision observations.
- Unocculted faculae in K dwarfs can appreciably alter transit depths around the Na D doublet.
- Band-averaged transit depth offsets at molecular bands for CH4, CO, CO2, H2O, N2O, O2, and O3 are not detectable for typically active FGK dwarfs.
- Inactive FGK dwarfs do not produce detectable stellar contamination features in transmission spectra.

## 2020ApJ...893L..43M

**Gold (human):**
- Applying one-dimensional retrieval models to spectra from atmospheres with different morning and evening terminator compositions can produce spuriously cold retrieved temperatures.
- One-dimensional retrievals of heterogeneous terminators can bias retrieved molecular abundances, including water, by more than an order of magnitude.

**Model:**
- Erroneously cold temperatures are retrieved from exoplanet transmission spectra when 1D atmospheric models are applied to planets with differing morning-evening terminator compositions.
- Retrieved abundances from 1D models are mostly biased by greater than 1σ and sometimes by greater than 3σ, with the most extreme biases for ultra-hot Jupiters.
- When morning-evening compositional differences affect prominent opacity sources, H2O abundances retrieved by 1D models can be biased by over an order of magnitude.
- The biases identified in 1D retrievals provide an explanation for the cold retrieved temperatures reported for WASP-17b and WASP-12b.
